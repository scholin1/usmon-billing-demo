USMON Insurance Billing: a guided test of the mock-up

For the mPower billing team: what to click, what you should see, and what to tell us

From the USMON Insurance Billing team · 10/26/2026 · Describes the mock-up build of 10/26/2026

Before you start

Read this part first. It takes about five minutes.

This is a clickable mock-up of the insurance billing module USMON is building. It runs on made-up data for two made-up clients, and nothing you do in it is saved anywhere but your own browser, so click freely. It is not the finished product: the screens show the words, the steps and the order of work we plan to build, and your reactions now will shape what gets built.

Set aside about 45 minutes. Work through the 15 tests in order, because the later ones build on the earlier ones. For each test, do the steps, compare what you see with what we expect, and mark yes or no. Dollar figures in the mock-up are illustrative; they are there so the screens look real, not to be checked.

1What you need

  • A computer with Chrome, Edge or Safari. Keep the window wide; the worklists are dense grids and a phone is too small for them.
  • The link: https://demo.usmon.com/app/. If it does not load, use the backup copy at https://scholin1.github.io/usmon-billing-demo/app/. The same files are on both.
  • No login. The mock-up opens with you already signed in as Pat Sample, an Insurance Biller. The page greets Pat by name and says "You are not in a view yet." The real module will sign you in with your own USMON account and roles.

2Four things to know about the mock-up

  • Reset demo. At the top right, click Settings ▾, then Reset demo at the bottom of the menu (in red), and confirm. Everything goes back to the start. Do this before you begin if someone else used the browser before you, and any time the demo gets into a strange state.
  • Familiar and Classic. The two buttons at the top right switch the look. Familiar is the new design. Classic is the look of the legacy USMON billing screens. Same screens, same data, same buttons; only the look and a few labels change. Test 15 is about this switch.
  • Two made-up clients. The mock-up holds XYZ Monitoring Co. and USMON Test Amb EEG. No real client, patient, payer, hospital or staff member exists in it. Names like "Mock Cigna Plan 1", "Specimen, Lorem" and "Sam Fixture" are invented on purpose, and so are the dollar amounts. Please do not type a real patient or client name into it; there is nothing to find.
  • The demo clock is fixed at 10/23/2026, so every tester sees the same dates and clocks. When a test asks you to pick a date, count from 10/23/2026, not from today.

3Words to know

Billing words the screens use, in plain terms. Each is also explained the first time a test uses it.

WordWhat it means here
PayerThe insurance company or program (Medicare, Medicaid, a commercial plan) the claim is sent to.
ClearinghouseThe service that carries claims to payers electronically and brings their answers back.
Box 33 (billing entity)The box on the claim form that names who gets paid. The mock-up picks it by a rule; the biller always confirms it before filing.
ScrubThe checks a claim must pass before it is filed: patient and insured, payer, diagnosis codes, service lines, rendering provider, Box 33.
Filing clockDays left to file the claim before the payer's time limit runs out. In the mock-up the limit is 90 days from the date of service for every payer.
835 (remittance)The payer's electronic statement of what it paid on a claim, line by line, and why. Also called an ERA or a remit.
EOBExplanation of benefits: the paper version of the same statement.
CARC codeClaim adjustment reason code, the payer's short code for why it paid less or nothing, for example CO-45 (charge is over the fee schedule) or CO-4 (modifier problem).
RecoupmentMoney the payer takes back on a later remittance.
Out-of-network, IDRA claim to a payer you have no contract with. Under the No Surprises Act it gets a 30-day open negotiation, then a short window to start Independent Dispute Resolution (IDR), where a neutral party picks one of the two offers.
USMON ArbitrationUSMON's own service that files and argues an IDR dispute for you.

9Work a denial

The denial screen says in plain words why the payer denied the claim and what to do about it. The appeal letter is drafted for you to edit.

What to do

  1. Click Denials in the top bar. Read the category buttons above the grid (All, Missing information, Modifier problem, Timely filing, Over fee schedule, Bundled, Medical necessity, Authorization, Wrong payer) and the column "What the payer is saying".
  2. Find Example, Pseudo (code CO-4) and click its Next button, Correct and resubmit.
  3. Read Why was this claim denied? and What to do next.
  4. Click the Overview tab and look at the Notes panel.
  5. Go back to the Denial tab and click Refile corrected claim.

What you should see

Why was this claim denied? lists the code CO-4, Modifier problem; What happened ("Denied in full on 05-10-2026; nothing was allowed"); What the payer is saying ("The procedure code is inconsistent with the modifier used, or a required modifier is missing"); What to do ("Correct the modifier on the service line, 26 for the professional read, TC for the technical part, then refile"); the remark code, the payer, the balance and an Appeal clock (180 days from the remittance). Under it, the denied lines from the remittance.

What to do next names the suggested move, Correct and resubmit, with a one-line reason, then shows the choices as cards: Correct and resubmit (with Refile corrected claim), and Appeal on the record with a letter already drafted ("To: Synthetic Medicare Replacement Plan 2, Appeals. On Overview, this older claim's Notes panel has two notes carried over from legacy, marked LEGACY, and rows marked SYSTEM written by the system, such as "Clearinghouse accepted the claim (999 and 277CA clean)" and "835 received: Denied CO-4". Untick "Show system notes" to hide those.

12Out-of-network negotiation and send to IDR

What we want to hear back

Your marks on the fifteen tests are the first half. The second half is these five questions. Short answers are fine; a sentence each is plenty.

Five questions

  1. What is missing that you use every day? A column, a screen, a report, a shortcut, a piece of information you always look for. Name it even if it seems small.
  2. Which screen would you start your day on? The welcome page, New Insurance Billing, Ongoing, Notes, Denials, Payments, something else?
  3. Was anything confusing? A word, a button, a step where you did not know what to do next, or something that happened that you did not expect.
  4. Familiar or Classic for you? And why.
  5. The one thing you would change if you could change only one.

Send your marks and your answers to Mike, by reply to the message that brought you this guide. A photo of a marked-up printout is fine; so is a few lines in an email. If you would rather talk it through, say so and we will set up a call.

!If something goes wrong

  1. A page looks stale or half drawn: reload the page once (F5, or Command-R on a Mac). Where you were and what you did are kept.
  2. The demo is in a strange state, or you want to start over: click Settings ▾ at the top right, then Reset demo at the bottom of the menu, and confirm. Everything goes back to the start.
  3. The site does not load: use the backup copy at https://scholin1.github.io/usmon-billing-demo/app/. The same files are on both addresses.
  4. A button does nothing: it may be greyed out on purpose. Rest your mouse on it; most greyed buttons explain why (for example "Approve at least one claim first").
