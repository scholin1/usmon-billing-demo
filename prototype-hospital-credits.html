A working prototype, not a picture of one. Every figure below is calculated from the entries beneath it, and every button does what it says — including refusing you when the answer should be no. The hospitals and invoice numbers are real records from one client's own data.

The screen that does not exist today

This list is the whole idea. USMON has 15,793 hospitals and a balance recorded for two of them. There is no account, no running total, and nowhere to answer the question "what do we owe this hospital, and what do they owe us." The right-hand column is money already sitting unhandled today — Springfield Regional alone is holding $177,550 across 262 overpaid invoices.

Five things to try — four are impossible in USMON today

  1. Record a payment of 2500 against the $1,850 outstanding. The cash clears the open invoices oldest first, and the extra $650 becomes credit on the account. Nobody classified it or raised a document. Today that $650 is recorded as "received" and disappears from view.
  2. Raise the next invoice. A real later invoice for this hospital. The credit is still sitting there, waiting, and visible — which is the whole point.
  3. Apply the credit to it. The balance settles and a line appears under the invoice naming the amount, the date and the person who did it. Today's equivalent record has no date and no user on it at all.
  4. Try to apply more credit than exists. It refuses. In the live system nothing checks: ten credit memos have been applied for $69,726 more than they were worth, and another $42,856 was applied against credit memos that do not exist.
  5. Reverse a payment. The original stays, struck through, with a correcting entry beside it. Nothing is ever deleted.

What this replaces

Today a biller chooses between eight menu entries — Daily, PO Waiting, Monthly, Weekly, Sent, Credit Memo, Write Off, Deleted — which are seven filters over one table plus one separate list. Nothing is hard to find because it is hidden; it is hard to find because you must already know which of eight doors it is behind. None of them can tell you what a hospital's balance is.
